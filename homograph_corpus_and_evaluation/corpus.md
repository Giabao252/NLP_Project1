# Homograph corpus: 50 texts

20 jokes + 20 paired literal rewrites + 10 additional non-jokes. Each J, N, or A entry is one text; annotations are not extra corpus texts.

## Selection and annotation

The supplied examples were selected and adapted to use identical written forms with distinct meanings. Same-spelling words may have the same pronunciation (mouse) or different pronunciations (bow, bass). Different-spelling sound puns are excluded. The repairs replace invalid or unclear wordplay; these are not claimed to be verbatim originals. The source number identifies the inspiration. Disk/disc, car tunes/cartoons, fast/fasten, and invented senses such as “bush up” were excluded.

Every rewrite retains the target spelling, but removes the comic reinterpretation. Some incidental words remain polysemous, as in ordinary language. A03–A05 deliberately mention two senses literally, making them harder non-jokes. Labels and explanations are author annotations, not independent human consensus. The earlier numeric age estimates have been removed. Target age is 12; sense-specific acquisition evidence is unavailable.

**J01 · Joke**

The baseball player asked for a bat. His teammate opened a cage and handed him a flying animal instead.

**N01 · Modified non-joke**

The baseball player asked for a bat. His teammate took a wooden one from the equipment bag and handed it over.

Annotation: **bat** — (1) a flying mammal; (2) a club used to hit a baseball. The request is for baseball equipment, but the teammate supplies the animal with the same name.

## Ten additional non-jokes

### A01

The library opens at nine each morning. Visitors can return borrowed books at the front desk before choosing something new to read.

### A02

Why were the chairs moved into the hallway? The classroom floor needed cleaning before the afternoon lesson began.

### A03

The museum displays a wooden baseball bat beside a photograph of a flying bat. The labels describe each exhibit separately.

### A04

The lesson defines current in two contexts: the movement of water in a river and the flow of electricity through a circuit.
